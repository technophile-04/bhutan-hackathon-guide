01· problem statement

credential verification.

academic equivalency for students who studied abroad.

verifying international academic qualifications — especially for students who studied in india or other countries — is still manual, slow, and inconsistent. different reviewers read the same qualification differently, so equivalency decisions vary. there's no reliable way to confirm a document with the issuing institution, and no central record of recognized institutions or past decisions, so every case starts from scratch and applicants rarely learn why they were accepted or rejected.

how might weautomate the verification, interpretation, and conversion of international academic credentials into bhutanese equivalency standards — with fairness, accuracy, and transparency built in?

bhutan bootcamp · druk land · 17 may 2026
02· problem statement

asset inventory.

tamper-resistant tracking for public procurement.

government agencies procure thousands of assets a year, recorded in centralized databases. those records can be edited, overwritten, or deleted, and audit trails are often incomplete. when an asset goes missing, is reassigned, or changes value, reconstructing its true history is a manual exercise — auditors spend weeks reconciling inconsistencies, and decision-makers operate on numbers they can't fully trust. as procurement spans more agencies, the gaps widen.

how might weturn government asset inventory and procurement from mutable database records into a transparent, tamper-resistant ledger, so officers, auditors, and oversight bodies can track every asset across its full lifecycle?

bhutan bootcamp · druk land · 17 may 2026
03· problem statement

age verification.

legal drinking age at the point of sale.

about 30.6% of bhutanese adults currently use alcohol, and a school-based study found roughly 24% of students under 18 were current users. the legal drinking age is 18 and vendors are required to check it — but enforcement is inconsistent in small shops, bars, and informal sales, where age checks come down to a shopkeeper's subjective judgement and weak identity checks.

how might wemake legal-drinking-age verification at the point of sale reliable and scalable, reducing underage access while making compliance easy for shopkeepers?

bhutan bootcamp · druk land · 17 may 2026
04· problem statement

the drug data gap.

evidence to drive substance-abuse policy.

substance abuse in bhutan is rising fast. heroin apprehensions jumped from 1 case in 2023 to 28 in 2024, and about 29 kg was seized in the first five months of 2025 alone. over 3,500 people were arrested for drug-related offences in 2025, many under 18. yet the last national drug use survey was in 2016, and data collection across agencies is fragmented and inconsistent — policymakers can't see the full scope.

how might westrengthen national data systems and research on substance abuse, so policy, prevention, and treatment run on current evidence instead of a decade-old survey?

bhutan bootcamp · druk land · 17 may 2026
05· problem statement

the last mile.

reaching the right person when data goes stale.

critical deliveries fail not because of bad roads but because location and contact data goes stale. flat numbers get re-numbered, owners move floors without updating records, landmarks are vague ("near the old water tank"), phone numbers change. legal notices, insurance documents, ambulances trying to find an emergency patient, and parcels all miss their target — because people, buildings, flat numbers, and contacts change frequently.

how might wereliably reach the right person at the right place — even when addresses, buildings, flat numbers, and contact details keep changing?

bhutan bootcamp · druk land · 17 may 2026
06· problem statement

ordered signing.

human-readable signatures, cryptographic proof.

getting a document signed, especially in a specific order, is slow, insecure, and hard to verify. wet-ink and thumb-stamp marks can be forged or tampered with, and proving a document is authentic and unaltered years later is hard when signatures aren't bound to real identities. a workable system needs both a clear visual signature people can see, and cryptographic non-repudiation underneath.

how might webuild a signing workflow that shows a clear visual signature while cryptographically proving who signed, in what order, and whether the file changed since?

bhutan bootcamp · druk land · 17 may 2026
07· problem statement

one-touch service.

public services for every citizen, literate or not.

public service delivery excludes a lot of people. literate citizens juggle portals, passwords, and physical cards; people who can't read can use a phone but not navigate forms or error messages. both fear fraud, and when officials help, their actions aren't traceable. cards get lost or forged, apps are heavy and need constant updates, and otps fail the people who need services most.

how might welet any citizen — whether they read or not — securely access any public service without cards or complex apps, using only their biometrics, with tamper-proof data and fully traceable helper actions?

i. rules of the road
· hackathon rules

hackathon rules.

the eight things to know before you submit.

1
deadline—2:30 pm, sunday 17 may. after that, no edits.
2
submit on speedrunethereum.com—one submission per team.
3
one problem statement per team—you can pivot during the build, but your final submission locks to one. judges score against just that one.
4
submit even if it's not finished—partial projects still count.
5
save your code often—push to github as you go, not in one big drop at the end.
6
ask mentors when stuck—that's what they're here for. don't sit silent. raise your hand.
7
show your project actually working—in your demo, click through the real flow. don't read slides at the judges.
8
tell us who in bhutan this helps—one real person, one real problem. that's the story judges remember.

submit something. anything is better than nothing.

ii. the demo
· a winning demo

a winning demo.

"the room remembers stories, not specs.

i.

show the product working.

not yourself talking. click through the real flow. skip metamask confirmations and loading screens. the judges should see it work, not hear about it.

ii.

tell one real story.

who is the bhutanese citizen whose life this improves? one person, one moment, one problem solved. specific beats abstract every time.

iii.

paint the two-year vision.

end with where this goes. "this is the beginning of an idea. help us see it." the room funds momentum, not finished products.

iv.

own your difference.

every team has the same ai tools. what you build on top of your core idea is what sets yours apart. be strategic about that one feature.
